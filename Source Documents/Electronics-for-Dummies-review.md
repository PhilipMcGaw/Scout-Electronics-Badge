# Review note: *Electronics For Dummies*

- **Reviewed:** 2026-11-06
- **Source file:** [Electronics for Dummies - converted.pdf](Electronics%20for%20Dummies%20-%20converted.pdf)
- **Title:** *Electronics For Dummies*, by Gordon McComb and Earl Boysen
- **Publication:** Wiley, copyright 2005
- **Length:** 433 PDF pages

## Review scope

Searchable text was extracted and compared with the Scout booklet, Leaders’ Guide, and existing blog drafts. I visually checked PDF pages 252–254 (printed pages 235–237), which show and describe one solderless breadboard. The rest of the book’s figures and photographs were not visually checked. This review records selected useful concepts and editorial cautions; it is not a technical endorsement of every instruction or example.

## Useful material and where it fits

- **Solderless breadboards:** Chapter 11 explains that internal metal contacts join groups of holes, allowing a circuit to be assembled and changed before soldering. Its photographed board shows one typical arrangement, with groups of five holes and power rows. The Scout booklet now introduces breadboard contacts and cautions that layouts and split power rows vary. The Leaders’ Guide adds a preparation check for the actual board and a reminder to power down before changing connections.
- **A possible practical article:** Breadboarding provides a hands-on route from schematic to prototype, then from prototype to a permanent board. A new [breadboard prototyping outline](../blog-drafts/breadboard-prototyping.md) records this idea. Use a specific, checked board and circuit for any detailed walkthrough.
- **Schematic and circuit concepts:** Chapters 1, 6, and 7 discuss complete circuits, components, and following schematic connections. These topics are substantially covered in the booklet already. The fundamentals draft can use breadboarding as the practical activity that connects the schematic with a real low-voltage circuit.
- **Multimeter and soldering coverage:** Chapters 8 and 9 overlap with the booklet’s soldering and multimeter sections. They do not justify copying more procedures; use current product instructions and the existing project safety guidance.

## Material not adopted

The book is from 2005 and is US-oriented. Do not adopt its mains-related demonstrations, US suppliers or conventions, dated product recommendations, or soldering, ESD, and troubleshooting advice without current, product-specific and UK-relevant verification. Its breadboard layout is an example, not a standard for all boards. Do not reproduce its figures or photographs; use an original or appropriately licensed visual if one is later requested.

## Disposition

The breadboard concept has been added to the learner booklet, Leader preparation notes, and electronics-fundamentals outline. A separate blog idea is indexed for possible development. Other basic topics mostly overlap with existing project material, so no further additions were needed.
