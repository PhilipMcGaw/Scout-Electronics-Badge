===== Slide content summary =====

These are attributed summaries of the course material as described in the supplied note. They are not independent technical verification or Philip’s personal reflections.

  * **Deck 01 — Introduction:** Introduces radiated and conducted emissions and immunity using a noisy source, a susceptible victim and a coupling path. Diagrams distinguish radiation through space from coupling via wiring. It gives a refrigerator/FM-radio interference example and attributes the 1967 USS //Forrestal// fire to a power surge during checkout, describing it as conducted emissions causing conducted susceptibility. That historical explanation is recorded as the slide’s account, not as an independently established cause. The deck also uses EME for electromagnetic effects, EEE/E3 for “EM Environmental Effects”, and EMR for “electromagnetic resilience”.
  * **Deck 02 — Dipoles and monopoles:** Discusses radiation from changing fields and displacement current, capacitive coupling, and unintended antennas formed by conductors with an RF potential difference. It extends the model to monopoles and nearby conductors, including power-cable conductors, and introduces bonding conductors to reduce potential differences; it notes that bond quality matters.
  * **Deck 03 — Loops and PCB returns:** Covers loop antennas, loop area, PCB trace return paths, slots in reference planes and cable loop area. It presents short return paths, routing power and return close together, and balanced twisted pairs as ways to reduce loop area or emissions.
  * **Deck 04 — Enclosures:** Covers seams, cable and ventilation openings, insulated painted contact surfaces and slots. It describes apertures as possible field-entry paths and radiating structures, and compares long slots with arrays of smaller openings such as honeycomb.
  * **Deck 05 — Switching noise:** Introduces time- and frequency-domain views, Fourier transforms, switching-frequency components and harmonics, and examples involving a switched-mode power supply and a digital clock. It discusses switching transitions, electromagnetic noise, electric-vehicle inverter circuitry, parasitic capacitance and parallel paths.
  * **Deck 07 — Warning signs and mitigations:** Presents switching frequencies and harmonics, rise time, and structures approaching a quarter wavelength as screening considerations. It gives an illustrative 30 MHz clock/1 ns rise-time example and lists layout, filtering and filter placement, shielding, cable routing, grounding and bonding as design measures. These are course rules of thumb; applicability to a particular product or standard requires separate analysis.
