====== Breadboard prototyping: from schematic to circuit ======

**Status:** Idea; outline not yet checked against a specific breadboard or circuit.

**Purpose:** Show how a solderless breadboard can help a beginner test a low-voltage circuit before soldering it, and how to avoid common connection mistakes.

===== Planned outline =====

- Start with a simple, Leader-checked low-voltage schematic and identify its source, components, and connections.
- Explain that a breadboard's internal metal contacts join groups of holes; use a diagram based on a selected board rather than implying every board has the same layout.
- Show how a centre trench can separate the two sides for a DIP IC, if the chosen board has one.
- Check the board's power rows: some are split or isolated. Confirm the layout from the manufacturer or with a continuity check while the board is unpowered.
- Place one component at a time and trace each connection back to the schematic. Keep wires short and label supply polarity.
- Check the circuit and component ratings before applying power. Switch the supply off before moving wires or parts.
- Observe and measure the working circuit, then change one feature at a time and compare the result with the prediction.
- Explain when a tested prototype is ready to move to stripboard or a PCB, and why the permanent build still needs its own inspection.

===== Checks before drafting =====

* Choose a real breadboard and verify its contact layout, power-rail breaks, and suitable wire sizes from its manufacturer information.
* Choose a simple circuit whose supply, current, and component ratings have been checked; avoid mains-powered projects.
* Use an original diagram or a properly licensed image. Do not reuse the figures from //Electronics For Dummies//.
* Keep the article practical and distinct from the fundamentals post by focusing on physical layout, tracing connections, and finding wiring errors.

===== Source notes =====

The 2005 //Electronics For Dummies// chapter on breadboards is useful background for prototyping and internal contact groups. Its pictured breadboard is only one layout, and its advice predates current products. See the repository's [[sites:scouts-electronics-badge:sources:electronics-for-dummies-review|source review]]; verify all board-specific details against the chosen product.

Iain Sharp’s 2010 [[https://lushprojects.com/absolutebeginners/|Electronics for Absolute Beginners]] course also pairs a schematic with a physical breadboard layout. Treat that layout as specific to the course’s board, not a universal pattern.
