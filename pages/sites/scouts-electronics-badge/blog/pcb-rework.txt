====== PCB rework: prototype track errors and solder-joint repairs ======

**Status:** Outline started; advanced article for adult electronics makers, not part of the Scouts booklet.

**Purpose:** Explain selected prototype-board repairs, their limits, and the checks needed before a repaired board is tested.

===== Planned outline =====

- Distinguish a temporary prototype repair from correcting the KiCad design and ordering a revised board.
- Identify the intended connection from the schematic and layout before modifying copper.
- Isolate an incorrectly routed track and check the cut with a continuity tester or multimeter.
- Add and route a suitable jumper wire; consider enamelled copper wire and preparation of its ends.
- Record Philip’s reported use of a small scalpel or drill bit, selected for track width, and his practice of securing the jumper wire flat with superglue to reduce snagging.
- Review solder-joint faults: too little solder, excess solder and bridges, disturbed joints, and cold joints.
- Inspect the repair, check intended continuity and unwanted shorts, and decide whether the prototype is safe to power.

===== Philip’s reported method and preferences =====

* Selects a small scalpel or drill bit according to the width of the track to cut, then checks continuity with a multimeter.
* Uses superglue to hold the jumper wire close to the board and reduce the chance of it catching.
* For solder joints, uses solder braid to remove excess SMD solder; adds flux and reheats disturbed joints; reheats the lead and pad together for a cold joint.

Check the adhesive’s suitability before recommending it generally. For enamelled wire, verify the specific insulation-removal method and ventilation requirements; do not present open-flame stripping as a general instruction. Keep the repair and all tools away from powered circuits.

===== Six-question knowledge check (working notes) =====

- **Too little solder:** the intended answer is a poor mechanical and electrical connection.
- **Incorrectly routed PCB track:** an enamelled-copper jumper can be a quick prototype repair, but first isolate the unwanted track and check for shorts.
- **Excess solder on an SMD joint:** remove the excess with solder braid.
- **Disturbed solder joint:** add flux and reheat the joint so the solder can reflow.
- **Cold solder joint:** reheat the component lead and PCB pad together so the solder can flow over both.
- **Too much solder between leads:** it can bridge connections and cause a short.

===== Checks before publication =====

* Verify wire, adhesive, solder, and tool guidance against the relevant manufacturers’ instructions and safety data.
* Make clear that a jumper does not correct the design; update the design and order a new board before production.
* Check for continuity where intended and shorts where not intended before applying power.
