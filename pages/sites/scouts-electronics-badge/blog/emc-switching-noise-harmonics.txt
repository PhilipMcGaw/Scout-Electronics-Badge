====== Switching noise, harmonics and edge rates ======

**Status:** Idea; adult-facing outline. Verify technical explanations and measurements against primary sources before drafting.

**Audience:** Adult electronics makers and technically experienced readers. Not a Scout construction activity.

===== Purpose =====

Explain why switching waveforms contain frequency components beyond their repetition rate, how fast transitions can increase high-frequency content, and how unintended coupling paths can turn switching activity into an EMC problem. Link the time-domain picture to what a spectrum analyser or oscilloscope FFT can show without treating either trace as a compliance result.

===== Planned outline =====

  * Start with a switching converter or digital clock as a recurring source, then trace the source, coupling path and affected circuit.
  * Compare a switching waveform in the time domain with its frequency-domain components. Explain the switching repetition frequency, harmonics, and why edge shape and rise/fall time influence the spectrum.
  * Describe parasitic capacitance and inductance as possible coupling paths. Relate loop geometry and return paths to the separate [[sites:scouts-electronics-badge:blog:pcb-layout-for-emc|PCB layout for EMC]] post.
  * Distinguish changing the switching frequency from changing edge rate; each can affect the spectrum and system behaviour differently. Do not offer a single rise-time formula or universal “slow edges are always better” rule without checking the device and design context.
  * Explain what an oscilloscope FFT and a spectrum analyser can each reveal. Discuss settings and limits only at a high level, with model-specific behaviour and measurement uncertainty clearly stated.
  * Close with a cautious troubleshooting sequence: identify a repeatable symptom, correlate it with switching operation, inspect likely coupling paths, make one controlled change, and compare measurements with a consistent setup.
